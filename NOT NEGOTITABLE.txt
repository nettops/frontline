# FRONTLINE — POLISH / ACCESSIBILITY / RETENTION PASS

You are working directly inside the Frontline repository.

This is a **polish, accessibility, discoverability, pacing, and retention pass** — NOT a rewrite and NOT a redesign of the underlying simulation.

The game already has a strong simulation foundation, strong writing/tone, meaningful consequences, and interconnected systems. The goal of this pass is to make the existing game easier to understand, smoother to operate, more discoverable, and more engaging over long careers.

---

# 1. CORE DIRECTIVE

Improve the player's experience without flattening or simplifying the simulation.

The target experience is:

> **"I always understand what happened, why it happened, what it means, and what I can do about it — without the game constantly interrupting me."**

Do NOT remove complexity simply because it is difficult.

Instead:

* expose complexity more clearly
* improve information hierarchy
* reduce unnecessary friction
* improve system discoverability
* improve late-game variety
* make consequences easier to understand
* preserve the existing tone and writing style
* preserve successful mechanics
* preserve meaningful player decisions

The game should still feel like a serious, systemic Mafia management simulation.

---

Start by thoroughly inspecting the existing codebase.

* organization/crew systems
* family/home systems

Do NOT immediately start modifying files.

First establish how the existing systems work and where the relevant logic lives.

Look for duplicated systems, contradictory state representations, brittle UI logic, and places where presentation is disconnected from the underlying simulation state.

# 4. INFORMATION HIERARCHY / EVENT PRIORITIZATION

One of the biggest problems identified in testing is excessive memo/event friction during Days ~180–300.

Not every event deserves equal interruption.

Create or improve an event priority model.

Use at least three levels:

## CRITICAL

Events that materially require player attention or an immediate decision.

Examples:

* raid
* assassination attempt
* major arrest
* indictment
* premises seizure
* crew execution
* major defection
* major territory loss
* major family crisis
* irreversible decision

These can interrupt the player's normal flow.

---

## IMPORTANT

Events that matter but do not necessarily require an immediate modal interruption.

Examples:

* loyalty decline
* increasing heat
* financial deterioration
* crew member entering custody
* developing investigation
* relationship deterioration
* approaching job deadline

These should appear in the daily digest and relevant system panels.

---

## INFORMATIONAL

Events that provide flavor, background, or minor context.

Examples:

* someone asking about the player
* minor rumors
* small social developments
* low-impact background activity
* recurring flavor events

These should NOT repeatedly interrupt day advancement.

They should be logged for later review.

---

# 5. STOP MODALS FROM BECOMING ADMINISTRATION

A major goal is to reduce:

> advance day → dismiss memo → click decision → dismiss → advance day → repeat

without removing meaningful events.

Where possible:

### Combine related informational events.

Instead of:

> Memo 1
> dismiss

> Memo 2
> dismiss

> Memo 3
> dismiss

Use:

### DAILY BRIEFING

**3 developments while you were away**

* Luca was questioned by police.
* The West Side collection route is producing less.
* Your legal exposure increased.

Then allow the player to inspect individual items if desired.

Do NOT combine events that require genuinely separate player decisions.

---

# 6. FIX AMBIGUOUS CREW / NPC LANGUAGE

This is a high-priority issue.

Never use ambiguous phrases such as:

> "X is out."

when the underlying state could mean multiple things.

The player must immediately understand whether someone is:

* active
* unavailable
* in custody
* being interrogated
* arrested
* cooperating
* defecting
* missing
* injured
* dead
* permanently departed
* temporarily unavailable

Use explicit terminology.

Examples:

BAD:

> Luca is out.

GOOD:

> Luca is in custody.

BAD:

> Carlo talked.

GOOD:

> Carlo spoke during questioning.

BAD:

> Lou is gone.

GOOD:

> Lou has defected.

or:

> Lou was killed.

or:

> Lou remains in custody.

The exact wording should fit the game's established writing style.

Do NOT make the writing generic or overly explanatory.

---

# 7. EVENT MESSAGES MUST COMMUNICATE STATE

Every important event should answer, directly or indirectly:

### WHAT HAPPENED?

### WHY DOES IT MATTER?

### WHAT STATE CHANGED?

### DO I NEED TO ACT?

For example:

> **LEGAL PRESSURE**
>
> Federal investigators expanded the case.
>
> Case exposure: 61 → 68
>
> Primary causes:
>
> * Witness testimony
> * Unexplained cash
> * Crew member interrogation
>
> No immediate action required.

This is substantially better than a vague:

> "The investigation continues."

The player should be able to understand the simulation without reverse-engineering it.

---

# 8. APPLY THE ODDS-SYSTEM PHILOSOPHY EVERYWHERE

The existing itemized odds presentation is one of the strongest parts of the game.

Use that as the design philosophy for other systems.

Where appropriate, expose:

* contributing factors
* positive modifiers
* negative modifiers
* current value
* previous value
* projected value
* relevant consequences

Examples:

### HEAT

**FEDERAL HEAT — 68**

+12 Witness testimony
+9 Unexplained cash
+7 Crew interrogation
−5 Political influence
−3 Legal containment

---

### LOYALTY

**LUCA — LOYALTY 72**

+8 Paid on time
+4 Territory share

−5 Missed family obligation

---

### ECONOMY

**WEEKLY CASH FLOW**

Revenue: $184,000
Operating costs: −$71,000
Legal reserve: −$30,000
Net: +$83,000

Do not expose every calculation everywhere.

Expose calculations when they help the player make better decisions.

---

# 9. IMPROVE DISCOVERABILITY OF EXISTING SYSTEMS

The audit/test report identified that the Home/Family system could remain effectively invisible until very late.

Do NOT solve this with giant tutorial popups.

Instead, create organic discovery.

Existing systems should naturally surface through gameplay.

For example:

* Family issues should emerge through family-related events.
* Home should become relevant through household decisions.
* Legal systems should become more visible as exposure rises.
* Crew systems should become more important as the organization grows.
* Political influence should become relevant as the player becomes more powerful.
* Advanced operations should emerge as the player's status increases.

The player should discover systems because their career creates reasons to use them.

---

# 10. DO NOT HIDE SYSTEMS JUST BECAUSE THEY ARE ADVANCED

Advanced systems can remain complex.

However, the player needs a clear indication that they exist.

Use subtle discoverability cues such as:

* new section becoming available
* contextual memo
* NPC conversation
* consequence
* notification
* status panel
* relevant decision
* new category appearing in an existing interface

Avoid:

> "CONGRATULATIONS! YOU UNLOCKED THE FAMILY SYSTEM!"

The game's tone does not support that style.

---

The game needs stronger long-term memory.

Create or improve a persistent **Career History / Timeline**.

* important crew changes
* arrests
* indictments
* major investigations
* executions

* betrayals

### CAREER — DAY 247

**Day 112** — Acquired first front
**Day 147** — Federal investigation opened
**Day 181** — Luca questioned
**Day 203** — Lou Rabinowitz executed
**Day 217** — Lost three fronts
**Day 241** — Household fractured

This should make the player's run feel like a **career**, not simply a sequence of numerical state changes.

# 13. LATE-GAME JOB VARIETY

The report identified repeated job types across 70+ late-game days.

Do NOT simply increase the number of randomly generated jobs.

Instead, make jobs evolve with the player's career.

Early career:

* collections
* muscle work
* small favors
* low-level enforcement

Mid career:

* larger collections
* negotiations
* protection
* territory disputes
* business opportunities
* crew management

Late career:

* political influence
* major negotiations
* high-value fronts
* strategic territory operations
* legal containment
* corrupt official relationships
* large-scale organizational decisions
* rival-family conflicts
* succession issues
* major investigations
* high-level financial decisions

The player's responsibilities should become increasingly managerial as their power increases.

The game should gradually shift from:

> "What job should I do today?"

toward:

> "What problems does my organization have today?"

That is much more appropriate for a boss-level Mafia simulation.

---

# 14. PRESERVE MEANINGFUL REPETITION

Do NOT eliminate recurring activities entirely.

Repetition is appropriate for a management simulation.

Instead, add:

* escalating stakes
* different modifiers
* different NPCs
* different consequences
* different risk profiles
* different political/legal circumstances
* different financial outcomes

The same basic activity can remain useful if its context changes.

---

# 15. IMPROVE CAUSAL FEEDBACK

When something bad happens, the player should have enough information to determine whether it was:

1. caused by their decision
2. caused by neglect
3. caused by another system
4. random
5. unavoidable

Do not reveal hidden information that is intentionally supposed to remain hidden.

Instead, expose enough evidence to allow reasonable deduction.

Example:

BAD:

> Your legal situation worsened.

BETTER:

> Your legal exposure increased.
>
> Recent factors:
> +11 unresolved witness pressure
> +8 unexplained cash
> +6 crew interrogation
>
> Your legal reserve remains below the recommended level.

This creates accountability.

---

# 16. DIFFICULTY SHOULD FEEL FAIR

Do NOT simply make the game easier.

The tester experienced genuine collapse and recovery, which is valuable.

Preserve:

* risk
* failure
* irreversible decisions
* resource pressure
* legal danger
* crew instability
* territory loss

Instead improve the player's ability to understand why they are losing.

The goal is:

> **"I lost because I made bad decisions."**

rather than:

> **"I lost because the game randomly decided to kill me."**

Audit major failure states for causal transparency.

---

# 17. FAMILY / HOME SHOULD HAVE WEIGHT WITHOUT BECOMING A CHORE

The family system should feel meaningful.

Do not turn it into another checklist.

Family events should occasionally:

* conflict with business
* influence decisions
* create emotional stakes
* impose opportunity costs
* create long-term consequences
* reflect the player's lifestyle

The important thing is that family should feel like a competing part of the player's life.

The player should sometimes have to choose between:

> **business**

and

> **personal life**

rather than simply completing family tasks.
