Pixel fleet — cars that belong and cars that do not

Nineteen vehicles, side-on in a fixed 64 × 24 cell, front left, in fourteen paints — every colour available to every car on the sheet. Unlike the armoury, most of this sheet is not art waiting for a system — the simulation is already full of cars and has simply never shown one. Three of the four groups below are load-bearing prose in src/ today.

The one that already exists

The unmarked is not a proposal. It is lawEnforcement.ts:61, which describes the surveillance stage as “Cars that do not belong. Your people are being followed and photographed.” — and world.ts:91 and events.ts:365, which both say the same thing again in the player's ear: “cars on corners that never had cars”, “parked where cars are not usually parked.”

The game already tells you a car is outside. It has no way to let you see it. One sprite, one row in the district panel when the investigation is at that stage, and a mechanic that currently only exists as a sentence becomes a thing on a street. That is the cheapest real win on any of these sheets.

One shape, six paints

Nothing here is redrawn. Paint is three hexes; glass, chrome, rubber and lamps are fixed. The plates the game would need are not drawn on purpose — a legible number on a car is a detail the simulation would then have to keep consistent, and it does not track one.
