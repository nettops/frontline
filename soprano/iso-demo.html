The Wasteland POV, with sprites instead of a mesh

Same camera philosophy section 2 asks for — 3/4, off-axis, zoomable — built the way the isometric CRPGs this style actually descends from built it: a projected grid, flat token sprites instead of 3D characters, and draw order standing in for a Z-buffer. Click the street to walk there. The sort itself is right — a token behind a building's far corner draws before it, one past the near corner draws after, verified directly rather than assumed. What isn't solved yet: at this building height, a walkable tile close to the wall doesn't always project far enough into the wall's own screen shape to read as a clean hide — a real gap, not a rounding error, and the honest next step rather than something to paper over here.

What's real here

The projection (worldToScreen), the click-to-walk pathing, the zoom/pan camera, and the depth sort are the actual technique — same one Fallout, the original Wasteland, and every Infinity Engine game shipped on before real-time 3D was affordable. This is not a toy version of it.

What's a placeholder

The "sprites" are flat colored tokens, not drawn characters. A real pass needs 8-16 painted or rendered directions per animation, per character — an art pipeline, not a code problem. Building the camera first, cheaply, is how you find out if 900 sprite frames are even worth commissioning before paying for one.

Where this beats the rig demo

At this camera distance a walk cycle is 4-6 pixels of bob, not nine joint angles — section 20's own rule, "don't spend effort on detail the player won't see," applied literally. Fewer moving parts, and the part that was actually fragile last time (limb geometry) isn't here at all.
